## Slang Term: Sempai
**English:** Sempai  
**Chinese (simp):** 老uggy (lǎohūyí)  
**Explanation:**  
In English, "sempai" is used to refer to someone who is older, more experienced, or of a higher rank within a specific context, such as in work or school. In Chinese slang, "老uggy" (lǎohūyí) is a humorous or mocking twist on this term, where "老" (lǎo) means "old" or "experienced," and "uggy" is a play on "sister-ugly" (sisu) in some online cultures. It's often used in a derogatory manner to mock someone who is overly confident or has a false sense of authority, making it clear that the speaker considers that person as less-than-savvy or outdated.
Example Usage:  
- In English: "He's my sempai, but he always acts like he knows everything."
- In Chinese (simp): "他对我是学长啦啦啦, 但他好像知道自己啥都知道似的。"
