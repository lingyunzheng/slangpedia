# Ratio (English-Chinese Slang)
**English:** Ratio
**Chinese:** 比例 (Bǐlì) / 报复比 (Bàofù bǐ) / 赛博比 (Sàibó bǐ)
**Explanation:**
- **Ratio**: In casual American English, "ratio" is often used to describe the comparison of someone's wealth, attractiveness, or any other desirable trait to another's. It can also refer to the ease of beating someone in a challenge, leading to phrases like "It's a 10 to me" (10 is a highly desirable rating on a scale).
- **比例 (Bǐlì)**: This word in Chinese literally means "proportion" but is similarly used to describe a favorably one-sided comparison, often in the context of wealth, beauty, or success.
- **报复比 (Bàofù bǐ)**: This term uses the more literal "报复" (bàofù, meaning "revenge" or "to get back"), but in slang, it's used to emphasize a "ratio" where one person or entity clearly has an advantage over another. For example, "他真人比照片的 ratio 很高" (Tā zhenrén bǐ piàoliàng de bǐlì hěn gāo, meaning "His real-life ratio to photos is very high").
- **赛博比 (Sàibó bǐ)**: Combining "赛博" (Sàibó, meaning "cyber" or "internet"), this term refers to an online comparison, often used in internet culture to compare one's achievements or assets to others. It can be more humorous or confrontational in a digital space. For example, "他的钱和他的人气在赛博比里都比别人多" (Tā de qián hé tā de rénhài zài Sàibóbǐ lǐ dōu bǐ bùrén duō, meaning "In the cyber ratio, his money and popularity are both more than others").
