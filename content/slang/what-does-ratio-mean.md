## Ratio (En/中)
### In English
**Ratio**: This term originally came from mathematics, meaning the relative size of two quantities. In contemporary slang, "ratio" is often used to describe a situation or person who is extremely bad or unfavorable, typically in social media arguments or online confrontations. The phrase "to go for a walk on the ratio" means to physically leave the scene, often because the interaction is not worth continuing. It's also used as a way to mock someone's argumentative logic or intelligence, suggesting that they should be "pardoned" because they're not worth engaging with. 
Example: "The response to his opinion was so bad, I think he's going for a walk on the ratio."
### In Chinese
**比赧 (bǐ nǎn)**：这个词本身起源于数学，指的是两个量的相对大小。在现代网络用语中，“比赧”通常用来形容一个极端糟糕或不利的情况，尤其是在社交媒体争论或在线矛盾中。这种表达方式意味着要么离开现场，因为互动不值得继续；要么用来嘲讽有人的论点或智能低下，暗示他们“值得原谅”，因为不值得与他们争论。
例句：“对于他的观点，回应太差了，我觉得他应该去‘去比赧’。”

Note: Chinese often uses non-standard slang or colloquialisms for such terms, and "比赧" is a creative translation to capture the essence of an English term not directly translatable.
