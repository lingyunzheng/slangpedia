# "Out of Pocket" in Chinese and English
## English
**Out of Pocket**: 
- **Literal Meaning**: This phrase literally means something that comes directly from your own pocket, hence it is you who bear the cost.
- **Slang Usage**: It is used to describe an expense that the speaker is personally responsible for. Often used in a way that sounds informal or conversational.
### Example:
- "If we go to that fancy restaurant, it will probably be out of pocket for us both."
## Chinese
**掏腰包 (tāo yāo bāo)**:
- **Literal Translation**: 盘子里掏钱 (pánlízi tāo qián) - "reach into the pocket" (literally, "reach into the dish to get money").
- **Usage**: This term is used in the same context as "out of pocket," meaning you are personally paying for something. It's commonly used in casual conversations and can also carry a hint of joking or lightheartedness.
### Example:
- "如果我们去那家高档餐厅，很可能是我们要掏腰包了。" (If we go to that fancy restaurant, it will probably be that we two are going to have to pay.)
