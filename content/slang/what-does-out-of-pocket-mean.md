# "Out of Pocket" Bilingual Slang Explanation
**English:** Out of pocket
- **Literal Meaning:** This phrase refers to expenses that must be paid directly from one's own funds.
- **Slang Explanation:** When you "go out of pocket," you're using your personal money for a payment that was supposed to be covered by someone else or an external source. It's often used when discussing unexpected expenses or when a fee wasn't expected to be your responsibility.
**Example Usage:**
- *English:* "I had to pay for my own lunch today because the company reimbursement got delayed – it was out of pocket for me."
- *Chinese:* "今天我必须自己付午饭的钱，因为公司的报销延期了——对我来说是出了口袋的钱。"
**Alternative Phrases:**
- *In English:* "On the hook," "self-paying"
- *In Chinese:* "认账" (rèn zhàng), "自掏腰包" (zì tāo yāo bāo)
