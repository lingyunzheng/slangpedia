## English: cooked
### Chinese: 烧焦了 (qiǎo jiāo le)

**Explanation:**

- **English:** When something is "cooked," it typically means that food has been prepared and is ready to eat. However, in slang, "cooked" can also mean someone or something has been tampered with or altered. For example, if a deal is "cooked," it suggests that the deal has been fixed or rigged in someone's favor, rather than being fair and legitimate.

- **Chinese:** "烧焦了" (qiǎo jiāo le) translates to "burned" or "charred." In this context, it's used metaphorically in Chinese slang to indicate that something has been manipulated or manipulated to the extent that it's no longer genuine. For example, if someone says a vote was "烧焦了," they mean the vote was fixed or skewed.

**Example Usage:**

- English: "The results of the election were cooked."
- Chinese: "这次选举的结果已经被烧焦了。" (Cǐ ci xué kě de jì lǜ yǐ jīng bèi qì jiāo le.)

**Alternative Phrases:**

- English: rigged, fixed, cheated
- Chinese: 作弊了 (zhǎo wèi le), 操纵了 (cā zōng le)
