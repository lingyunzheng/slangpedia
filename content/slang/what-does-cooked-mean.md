```markdown
## English: Cooked
### Chinese: 水了 (Shuǐ le)
**Explanation**: 
When something is "cooked" in English slang, it can mean plans have been compromised or that someone has been tricked or deceived. In Chinese slang, saying something or someone is "水了" (shuǐ le) means it's fake, staged, or not genuine. It suggests that there's a hidden agenda or that something doesn't add up.
**Example Usage**:
- **English**: "I thought that was a good idea, but things got cooked when the boss walked in."
- **Chinese**: 我以为那是个好主意，结果老板走进来后一切都水了。 (Wǒ yǐwèi nà shì gè hǎo zhǔyì, juéméng bāozhǎng zǒngjìn lái hòu yīnggù dōu shuǐ le.)
- Translation: I thought that was a good idea, but after the boss walked in, everything turned out to be fake.
- **English**: "He told me he lost his job, but it seems he just cooked the story to get sympathy."
- **Chinese**: 他跟我说他失业了，但看来他是编了个故事想要博取同情。 (Tā wògesēn tā shīyòng le, dàn kànlái tā shì biān gěi gè gùshì yào xiǎng bòqǔ tóngtīng.)
- Translation: He told me he lost his job, but it seems he invented the story to get sympathy.
```
This markdown gives a clear definition and usage context for the slang term "cooked" in both English and its Chinese equivalent "水了 (shuǐ le)".
