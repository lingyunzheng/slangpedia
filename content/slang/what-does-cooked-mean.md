## English: Cooked
### Chinese: 烧utow (niu tòng) literally "very hot"
**Explanation:**
- **English:** Something or someone is in a very bad situation, often with no way to escape, like being cooked in a pot. It can also imply that a scheme, plan, or situation is completely foiled or that an individual is in a very uncomfortable or difficult position.
- **Chinese:** “烧utow” (literally “very hot”) is often used in informal or colloquial settings to convey the same meaning. It is a playful way of saying that something or someone is in a dire situation or that a plan has failed completely. 
**Example Usage:**
- **English:** "He's cooked; the cops are on his trail."
- **Chinese:** "他烧utow了，警察已经追到他头上来了。" (Tā niu tòng le, jǐng chá yǐjīng zhuī dào tātóu shàng lái le.)
## English: Cooked (as in food)
### Chinese: 烧烂 (shāo làn) literally "burnt to a pulp"
**Explanation:**
- **English:** Food that has been overcooked and is not appetizing.
- **Chinese:** “烧烂” (literally “burnt to a pulp”) describes food that has been cooked to the point of being tough, dry, or unpalatable. It can be used to criticize the quality of cooking or to express disappointment with the meal.
**Example Usage:**
- **English:** "The chicken was completely cooked; it was dry and flavorless."
- **Chinese:** "这只鸡被煮得烧烂了，又干又没味道。" (Zhège jī bèi zhǔ dé shāo làn le, yòu gān yòu méi wèi dào.)
