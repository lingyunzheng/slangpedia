```markdown
## Slang Explanation for "cooked"
**English:** cooked  
**Chinese (Cantonese):** 椢 להם都已经做完 (yaa6 go2 zau5 daai6) / 椢yectosafa (yaa6 jyut6 yaa6 fat6)  
**Explanation:** When someone says something is "cooked," it means that the plan or idea is ready, finished, or finalized. In Chinese, it can be translated as "已经做完" (yaa6 go2 zau5 daai6) which literally means "already finished" or "已经搞掂" (yaa6 jyut6 ge0 daai6) which means "already handled well" or " nailed it."
### Examples:
- **English:** The project is cooked.
- **Chinese (Cantonese):** 企划已经搞掂了。 (gai6 waah6 yaa6 go2 ge0 daai6 le5) 
This usage is common in a business or casual setting where the outcome is seen as certain or final.
```

```markdown
## Slang Explanation for "cooked"
**English:** cooked  
**Chinese (Mandarin):** 煮熟 (zhǔ shú) / 已经搞定 (yǐjīng gǎodiǎn)  
**Explanation:** When something is "cooked" in English slang, it generally means that something is finished, resolved, or ready for use. In Chinese, "煮熟" (zhǔ shú) idiomatically means to be fully prepared or completed, while "已经搞定" (yǐjīng gǎodiǎn) means it has already been handled or concluded in a satisfactory manner.
### Examples:
- **English:** The event is cooked.
- **Chinese (Mandarin):** 这个活动已经搞定。 (zhège huódòng yǐjīng gǎodiǎn.)
This phrase is useful in both casual and professional contexts, indicating that something is completed or finalized.
```
These explanations and examples should help clarify the usage of "cooked" in both English and Chinese!
