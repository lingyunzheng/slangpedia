## "Cooked" in English & Chinese
- **English Slang: "Cooked"**
- *Explanation:* Generally used to describe something or someone that is of high quality or in a complete state of readiness.
- *Example Usage:* "This meal is really cooked - it's delicious and perfectly prepared."
- *Chinese Equivalent:* "炖透" (dùntòu) - Literally meaning "cooked thoroughly," used to describe food that is perfectly prepared or an excellent job/presentation.
- *Example Usage:* "这道菜炖透了，非常美味。" (Zhè dào càilà yùndòu le, fēicháng měiměi.) - This dish is cooked thoroughly, it's very delicious.
- **English Slang: "Cooked the Books"**
- *Explanation:* Term used when someone tampered with or falsified records or accounts to create a misleading impression of financial or other data.
- *Example Usage:* "He was caught trying to cook the books and embezzle money from the company."
- *Chinese Equivalent:* "算账糊弄" (suànzhàng húlòng) - Literally meaning to "brainwash the accounts," referring to falsifying financial records or accounts.
- *Example Usage:* "他们被发现试图算账糊弄以从公司诈取资金。" (Tāmen bèi fàxiàn shìurgeryì suànzhàng húlòng yǐ cóng gōngsī zhàqǔ jīzī.) - They were found trying to brainwash the accounts in order to defraud the company of funds.
- **English Slang: "Cooked the Game"**
- *Explanation:* Used to describe a situation where one has managed to manipulate the outcome of something, usually a game, to their advantage.
- *Example Usage:* "We all knew he had cooked the game and was going to win."
- *Chinese Equivalent:* "匪夷所思" (fēiyísuǒsī) - Primarily used to describe something unexplainable, but can be used to imply unfair manipulation of a game.
- *Example Usage:* "我们都知道他早就已经匪夷所思地操控了比赛，肯定会赢。" (Wǒmen dōu zhīdào tā zdg7u zǎo zěnme yǐjǐng bǐng guǎngle bǎibiǎo，kěyǐnìng huì yíng.) - We all knew he had unfairy had already manipulated the game and was sure to win.
