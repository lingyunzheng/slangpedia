## English: Cooked
### Chinese: 煮熟了 (zhǔ shú le) / 烤熟了 (kǎo shú le)
**Literal Meaning:** Literally translated, this phrase means "cooked" or "roasted until done."
**Bilingual Slang Explanation:**
- **English:** "Cooked" is slang that means something is fully done or completed, often in a positive or enthusiastic way.
- **Chinese:** "煮熟了 (zhǔ shú le)" or "烤熟了 (kǎo shú le)" means that something has been thoroughly finished or settled. It can imply that a situation or task is now complete or that someone is fully committed to a plan.
**Examples:**
- **English:** "The party was cooked! Everyone had a great time, and the food was delicious."
- **Chinese:** "聚会搞定了！每个人都玩得很开心，食物也很美味。" (Huìjù gǎodìng le！Dúèr jiē dōu wán de hěn kāixìnɡ, shíwù yě hěn měiwěi.)
- **English:** "We've cooked up a plan to surprise everyone on their birthday."
- **Chinese:** "我们已经想好了一个惊喜方案，要在他们生日那天实施。" (Wǒmen yǐjīng xiǎnghǎo le yígè jīngxǐ fāng'àn, yào zài tāmen shēngrì nàtiān shítí.)
In both cultures, "cooked" or its Chinese equivalent can also be used metaphorically to describe when a plan or strategy is thoroughly thought out and ready to be put into action.
