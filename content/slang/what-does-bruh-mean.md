```markdown
## Bruh (English)
**Meaning**: Used to express confusion, disbelief, or mild annoyance. It's often used to respond when something is deemed extremely ridiculous or odd.
**Example**:
- **English**: "I just saw a car drive through a stop sign and then park in front of the grocery store. Bruh!"
- **Literal Translation (not direct, as slang doesn't always translate literally)**: "I just saw a car break the rule and park somewhere unexpected. Bruh!"
## 布鲁 (Chinese, Pinyin: bù lǔ)
**Meaning**: 布鲁 (bù lǔ) is a phonetic equivalent used in Chinese to mimic the sound of "bruh." It is typically used in similar contexts to express confusion, disbelief, or mild annoyance. Since it's a phonetic adaptation, it doesn't have a direct or literal Chinese equivalent.
**Example**:
- **Chinese**: “刚看到一辆车闯红灯然后停在了超市前面。布魯！”
- **Translation**: “刚看到一辆车闯红灯然后停在了超市前面。布魯！”（我刚看到一辆车闯交通信号灯然后停在了超市前面。布魯！）
```
In Chinese, the term "布鲁" (pinyin: bù lǔ) is used to mimic the sound of the English slang "bruh" and is similarly used to convey feelings of disbelief or mild exasperation.
