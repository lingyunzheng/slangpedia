## French (frFR) Slang Explained
### Casse-pipe
**English:** Drag, burden  
**Explanation:** A task or situation that is tiresome or difficult. Example: "C'est vraiment un casse-pipe de devoir nettoyer la cave." (It's really a drag to have to clean the basement.)
### Tchatouille
**English:** Wimp, sissy  
**Explanation:** A person considered feminine or weak, often used in a teasing manner. Example: "Il est devenu un vrai tchatouille à la retraite." (He's become a real sissy in retirement.)
### Boum
**English:** Party, event  
**Explanation:** A casual way to refer to a party or any social gathering. Example: "On va faire un boum dimanche soir." (We're going to throw a party Sunday evening.)
### Ploum
**English:** Moron, idiot  
**Explanation:** Used to refer to someone who is unintelligent or foolish. Example: "Il a fait une remarque tordante, mais c'est tant mieux, on voit bien qu'il est un vrai ploum." (He made a witty remark, but that's fine, we can see he's a real moron.)
### Phrase d'argot
**English:** Slang expression  
**Explanation:** An informal expression not commonly used in standard French. Example: "C'est du grand n'importe quoi!" (It's complete nonsense!)
---
These slang terms reflect the casual and vibrant language used in everyday conversation in France.
