# French (frFR) Slang Explained
## 1. Balancer
- **French**: Balancer
- **English**: To balance (informal use)
- **Explanation**: Originating from a verb that can mean "to weigh" or "to distribute," "balancer" is often used in a more colloquial context to mean making things even, settling a score, or balancing the scales. For example, "On va la balancer" could mean "We're going to settle this score."
## 2. Bouffer
- **French**: Bouffer
- **English**: To eat (hyperbole)
- **Explanation**: This term is used in a way that goes beyond just eating for real. It’s often used to emphasize that you ate a really big meal, sometimes exaggeratedly. For instance, "Hier j'ai bouffé une vingtaine de croissants" means "I ate twenty croissants yesterday," which could be simply a hyperbolic way of saying "I ate a lot."
## 3. Pognon
- **French**: Pognon
- **English**: Money
- **Explanation**: This term is a slang word for money that sounds a bit rough or informal. It can be used in various contexts but is more commonly heard in casual conversation. For example, "Il a pas beaucoup de pognon" means "He doesn't have much money."
## 4. Pute
- **French**: Pute
- **English**: Whore
- **Explanation**: While this is a very crude term with strong connotations, it’s often used informally as a slang term for a prostitute or someone who is sexually promiscuous. However, it’s important to use this word carefully as it can be offensive. For example, "Elle est toute pute" means "She’s a total slut."
## 5. Mechisme
- **French**: Mechisme
- **English**: Mechism (a playful twist)
- **Explanation**: This is a playful and somewhat internet-inspired slang term that combines "méchanceté" (malice) with "isme" (ism). It refers to a person who is always being malicious or mean. While not common, this term shows how French speakers blend words to create new slang terms. For example, "Il est un mec très mechisme" means "He’s a very mean person."
## 6. Ubermétro
- **French**: Ubermétro
- **English**: Ubermétro (a mix of "uber" and "sous-métro" which means "underground")
- **Explanation**: This term is a mix of "uber" from English and "sous-métro" meaning "subway." It refers to an ultra-cool or overprivileged person who travels in style, like taking an Uber in the subway. For example, "Elle est un vrai Ubermétro" means "She’s a real Ubermétro."
## 7. Pingouin
- **French**: Pingouin
- **English**: Penguin
- **Explanation**: In this slang, pingouin (penguin) is used to describe a person who is seen as awkward or geeky, often in a humorous way. For example, "Il est un peu pingouin" means "He’s a bit of a penguin" (awkward or nerdy).
## 8. Benêt
- **French**: Benêt
- **English**: Dimwit (mildly offensive)
- **Explanation**: This is a mild insult referring to a person who is perceived as simple-minded or unintelligent. It’s somewhat softer than some other insults. For example, "C'est un benêt" means "He’s a dimwit."
## 9. Choupette
- **French**: Choupette
- **English**: Petname (affectionate)
- **Explanation**: Used as an affectionate term, similar to "sweetheart," "honey," or "darling." For example, "Chophy la choupette" means "Cophy, my little sweetheart."
## 10. Boule policy
- **French**: Boule policy
- **English**: Boule policy (slang adaptation)
- **Explanation**: This term is a humorous adaptation of "ball policy," which refers to a policy where balls (in a sports context) are handled with care. In French slang, it can refer to something being handled with extra care or sensitivity, especially in a joking manner. For example, "On a obrigovies une boule policy" means "We have a very carefull policy," where "obrigovies" is a made-up word.
