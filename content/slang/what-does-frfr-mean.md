# French Slang Explained
## Franglais
A mix of French and English words often used in everyday conversation. It's like when you mix your favorite toppings with ice cream to make it even more yummy and unique!
### Exemple:
- "**Franglais**" - *Un mélange de mots français et anglais dans la conversation jour à jour.*  
- **English Equivalent**: A blend of French and English in daily talk.
## Puteaux
An exclamation of disbelief or frustration, similar to saying "What's wrong with you?" in English.
### Exemple:
- "Tu m'as déçue, putectime !" (Tu m'as déçue, puté-coeur !) - *Je suis déçue par toi.*  
- **English Equivalent**: "You've disappointed me, what's wrong with you?"
## Le Bébé
Lovingly used to refer to something or someone, akin to "sweetheart" or "darling" in English.
### Exemple:
- "Ah le bébé, tu es la plus belle chanteuse que j'aie jamais entendue!" - *Oh my sweetheart, you are the most beautiful singer I have ever heard!*
- **English Equivalent**: "Oh my love, you are the most beautiful singer I've ever heard!"
## Se Wetter
An informal way to express a feeling of uneasiness or nervousness, similar to saying "I'm feeling a bit on edge" in English.
### Exemple:
- "J'ai une réunion importante en début d'après-midi, et j'se wette que je vais être débordé..." - *I have a big meeting in the afternoon, and I feel pretty stressed...*
- **English Equivalent**: "I have a big meeting in the afternoon, and I'm feeling a bit on edge..."
## Tée
An affectionate way to refer to a close friend, somewhat like saying "sweets" in English.
### Exemple:
- "Alors tée, ça fait combien de temps qu'on ne s'est pas vus ! Qu'est-ce que tu up !!" - *Hey sweetie, how long has it been since we've seen each other! What's new!*
- **English Equivalent**: "Hey buddy, how long has it been since we've seen each other! What's new!!"
## Fringois
Refers to someone who speaks and dresses in a stereotypically French way, often implying a certain level of pretentiousness or snobbery.
### Exemple:
- "T'es un vrai fringois avec ta chemise à carreaux et ta moustache folle !" - *You're such a French stereotype with your checked shirt and wild mustache!*
- **English Equivalent**: "You're such a stereotypical French guy with your checked shirt and wild mustache!"
## Zozos
A term used to describe clothes or fashion, similar to "posh" or "fashionable" in English.
### Exemple:
- "Elle a acheté de nouvelles zozos pour aller à la fête." - *She bought new stylish clothes to go to the party.*
- **English Equivalent**: "She bought some new fashionable clothes for the party."
