# French Slang Explanations
## Franchement
- **French**: Franchement
- **English**: Honestly, really, seriously
- **Explanation**: Used to emphasize a statement or show strong agreement. It's akin to saying "Honestly" or "Really" in English.
## Pas mal
- **French**: Pas mal
- **English**: Not bad, okay
- **Explanation**: Used to say something is good but not great. It's a mild compliment or expression of approval.
## Chaud devant
- **French**: Chaud devant
- **English**: Pass the heat! (figuratively, meaning "Get the show on the road")
- **Explanation**: Typically used in theater or public speaking to start a performance, meaning it's time to begin. Figuratively, it can also mean "Let's get started" or "Let's go."
## Des mains de maître
- **French**: Des mains de maître
- **English**: Masterful hands
- **Explanation**: Used to describe someone who does something with extraordinary skill, literally meaning "hands of a master."
## Chambouler
- **French**: Chambouler
- **English**: To turn upside down, to reorder, to plunge into confusion
- **Explanation**: Used to express that something has caused a lot of changes, confusion, or chaos.
## C'est la vie
- **French**: C'est la vie
- **English**: That's life, that's how it goes, such is life
- **Explanation**: Used to acknowledge that a situation is just the way life goes, often to express resignation or acceptance.
## P'tit salop
- **French**: P'tit salop
- **English**: Little bastard (informal and not very polite)
- **Explanation**: Informal and slightly offensive, used to refer to someone in a derogatory manner.
