## French Slang Explanations (fr/fr)
### 1. Boulot
- **English:** Job, work
- **Explanation:** While "boulot" can literally translate to "work" or "job", it often carries a more casual or colloquial connotation, similar to how the English "mog" or "grind" might be used. It can imply drudgery or monotonous labor. Example: "Je préfère faire du boulot sans être payé que de faire des choses sympas et être payé pour rien."
### 2. Mamie
- **English:** Granny, grandma
- **Explanation:** "Mamie" is the informal term for grandmother, but in slang, it can be used humorously or as a term of endearment, often with a touch of comedic exaggeration. Example: "Ce chien est aussi drôle que sa mamie lorsqu'elle raconte ses jeunes années!"
### 3. Foutez-le-chien
- **English:** Give it up, quit it
- **Explanation:** This phrase literally means "f*ck the dog", but it is used to tell someone to stop, get lost, or to give up on something. It's a strong and direct way to tell someone to go away or to stop doing something. Example: "Cesse de jacasser, foute-le-chien!"
### 4. C'Dort
- **English:** It's knocked out, it's down
- **Explanation:** This slang term is often used in the context of a fight or a competition. It's used to describe something or someone who has been knocked out or defeated. Example: "Oh non, le rival de mon équipe, c'dort maintenant, c'est la fin!"
### 5. Chelou
- **English:** Strange, weird, cool
- **Explanation:** "Chelou" can be used both to describe something as strange or weird, but it can also be a term of approval for something that's considered cool or quirky. Example: "Cette tunique est trop chelou, j'adore!"
### 6. Salop
- **English:** Scumbag, jerk
- **Explanation:** "Salop" is a strong term of abuse, similar to the English "scumbag". It's used to insult or express frustration towards someone. Example: "Ce type est vraiment salop, il a tout gâché."
### 7. P'tit
- **English:** Little
- **Explanation:** "P'tit" is an informal way to say "little" or "small". It is often used as an affectionate way to address people, especially in the diminutive form. Example: "P'tit vieux, regarde bien ta défaite, elle est belle!"
### 8. Ouais
- **English:** Yeah, sure
- **Explanation:** This is a simple yet versatile word used to express agreement or to acknowledge something, similar to "yea" or "yeah". Example: "Tu veux qu'onaille au cinéma ce soir? Ouais, pourquoi pas!"
### 9. Crade
- **English:** Filthy, shabby
- **Explanation:** "Crade" describes something as dirty, nasty, or shabby, but it can also be used with a sense of excitement or enthusiasm when referring to something that's difficult or challenging. Example: "C'est un défi crade, mais j'aime ça!"
### 10. Balance
- **English:** Go away, stop it
- **Explanation:** While "balance" is borrowed from English, in French slang, it can mean to stop or to go away, similar to "cut it out" or "hit the road". Example: "Balance avec tes tweets débiles, ça n'intéresse personne!"
