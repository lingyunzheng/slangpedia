```markdown
### Bilingual Slang Explanations for French (frfr)
1. **Coucou**
- **French:** Coucou
- **English:** Hey there! (used as a casual greeting)
- **Explanation:** A common informal way to greet someone, similar to "Hey" or "Hi" in English.
2. **Marrant(e)**
- **French:** Marrant(e)
- **English:** Funny/Lame (depending on context)
- **Explanation:** Used to describe something as amusing or, sometimes ironically, not so impressive. Context is key to determine the exact meaning.
3. **C'est cool**
- **French:** C'est cool
- **English:** It's cool
- **Explanation:** Used to agree with something or express that everything is alright, similar to saying "It's fine" or "It's okay."
4. **Trop copain(e)**
- **French:** Trop copain(e)
- **English:** Too buddy-buddy (informal and sometimes condescending)
- **Explanation:** Used when someone is being extra friendly or familiar in an inappropriate way. Can be interpreted as a complaint or criticism.
5. **Du con** / **De la conne**
- **French:** Du con / De la conne
- **English:** Jerk / B*tch (informal and vulgar)
- **Explanation:** A very vulgar insult used to describe someone who is rude, immoral, or considerate of others. Use with extreme caution.
6. **Bouge pas**
- **French:** Bouge pas
- **English:** Don't move (completely still)
- **Explanation:** Used to tell someone not to move from their current position, often in a commanding or urgent way.
```
Note: The last example ("Bouge pas") is generally not used in a slang sense to mean "Too buddy-buddy." It’s a different expression. The more vulgar "Du con" / "De la conne" should be used cautiously.
