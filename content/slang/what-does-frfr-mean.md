# French Slang Explained
## 1. **C'est la guerre**
- **French**: C'est la guerre
- **English**: It's war
- **Explanation**: This phrase is used to express that it's a tough situation where conflict or intense competition is unavoidable or expected.
## 2. **Cherche le pépin**
- **French**: Cherche le pépin
- **English**: Look for the problem
- **Explanation**: Literally meaning "look for the pea," it's used when someone is trying to create or find a problem, often in a situation that's already difficult.
## 3. **En é-equivaut à**
- **French**: En é-equivaut à
- **English**: Same as
- **Explanation**: A humorous way to say that something is "the same as." This phrase is typically used in a playful context, imitating people who can't pronounce certain sounds correctly.
## 4. **Y a pas de souci**
- **French**: Y a pas de souci
- **English**: No problem
- **Explanation**: Used to reassure someone that a request or task will be taken care of without any issues.
## 5. **Pour la galle**
- **French**: Pour la galle
- **English**: For the gall, to hell, to blazes
- **Explanation**: An expression used to convey extreme frustration or anger, similar to saying "to hell with it" in English.
## 6. **Abuser du luxe**
- **French**: Abuser du luxe
- **English**: To abuse luxury
- **Explanation**: This phrase is used to describe someone who is excessively wasteful or indifferent to the costs of luxury items.
## 7. **Faire des pelles**
- **French**: Faire des pelles
- **English**: To dig a hole
- **Explanation**: This idiom means to put in a lot of effort to achieve something, often playfully to describe someone going to great lengths.
