## Franglais: [Fagne](https://fiche-slang.fr/fagne/)  
**Fagne**:  
*English Equivalent:* Sh*t (colloquial, vulgar)  
- *Meaning:* Used to describe something extremely difficult or messy. It often carries a slightly vulgar tone and can be used to express frustration. For example, you might say "J'en ai vraiment une fagne avec cette tâche" to convey that a task is really frustrating or difficult.

**Chelou**:  
*English Equivalent:* Wonky / Odd (colloquial)  
- *Meaning:* Used to describe something that is odd, strange, or just doesn't fit in, often with a slightly approving tone. It can also be used as an adjective for someone who is strange or quirky. **Fainéant**:
*English Equivalent:* Slacker (colloquial, slightly derogatory)  
- *Meaning:* Used to describe someone who won't or can't do their work or tasks, usually in a derogatory way. It implies laziness or unwillingness to put in effort, e.g., "C'est un vrai fainéant qui n'aille pas travailler" which suggests someone is a lazy person and doesn't want to work.
## Franglais: [Naze](https://fiche-slang.fr/naze/)
**Naze**:  
*English Equivalent:* Bogus / Scram (colloquial, informal)  
- *Meaning:* Used to express disapproval or disappointment about something or someone. It can also be used to describe something that is bad or unpleasant. For example, "C'est naze" can mean that something is very bad or disappointing.

**La gueule du loup**:  
*English Equivalent:* The wolf's mouth (colloquial)  
- *Meaning:* This phrase is a figurative expression meaning "honest to God" or "I swear."
