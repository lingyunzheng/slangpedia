## English: Locked In
### Chinese: 跳不出坑 (tiào bù chū kěng) or 挨着天花板睡 (ái zhe céng tiān shuì)
**Meaning:**
- **Locked In:** When someone is so deeply committed to a decision, situation, or commitment that they can't easily or realistically change their mind or course of action, often due to sunk costs or effort already invested.
**Examples:**
- In English: "I'm locked in on this project and can't really back out now, since I've already invested so much time and money."
- In Chinese: "我已经被这个项目锁定了，已经投入了太多的时间和资金，退出就太难了。" (Wǒ yǐ jīng bèi zhè ge xiàng mù suǒ lìn le, yǐ jīng tūn rù le tài duō de shí jiān hé jī jīn, tuì chū jiù tài nán le.)
**Explanation:**
- **跳不出坑 (tiào bù chū kěng):** This literally means "can't jump out of the pit." It describes a situation where someone feels they can't leave a situation they're in, much like how摆脱不了困境 (pái tōu bù liǎo jìng kùn) means being trapped or unable to escape from a difficult situation.
- **挨着天花板睡 (ái zhe céng tiān shuì):** This phrase literally means "sleeping with the ceiling touching your head," symbolizing being completely and utterly stuck in a situation, unable to change or move. It's often used when someone is deeply committed to something and can't leave it easily.
