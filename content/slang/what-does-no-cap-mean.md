## English: No Cap
### Chinese: 没带帽 (Mò dài mào) / 真的 (Zhē de)
**Explanation:** 
- **English:** Used to emphasize something is genuine, real, or not exaggerated. It's a way to assert that you are not "kidding" or that you are speaking truthfully. It's part of casual, informal language.
- **Chinese:** 
- 没带帽 (Mò dài mào) is a more technical phrase meaning "not wearing a hat," which is creatively used to convey the same meaning as "no cap."
- 真的 (Zhē de) is a straightforward translation meaning "really" or "truly," used to confirm the sincerity of what is being said.
Example Usage:
- **English:** "I really landed that job, no cap."
- **Chinese:** "我真的得到了那份工作，没带帽。" (Wǒ zhē de dé dào le bàn fèn gōngzuò, mò dài mào.)
