## Valid (USEnglish/UKEnglish)
**USEnglish:**
- **Meaning:** Something that is true or correct; legitimate.
- **Example:** "That ticket you bought isn't valid, it expired last month."
**UKEnglish:**
- **Meaning:** Similar to the US usage, it refers to something being genuine or lawful.
- **Example:** "This Membership card isn't valid until further notice, as they've changed the membership rules."
**Bilingual Slang Explanation:**
In both US and UK English, "valid" isn't necessarily slang, but when used in casual or informal contexts, it can imply something is "legit" or "real deal," as if to emphasize its truthfulness or legality.

**Cantonese Slang (/System U):**
- **Meaning:** 喺现实中可用 (Valid in the real world), 比较贴近事实或规则 (More closely aligned with facts or rules).
- **Example:** "呢張車票既年期唔夠，已經過期咗，沒有咗較现实可用嘅效用。" (This ticket's validity is not enough; it has expired, no longer being usable in reality.)
**Hokkien Slang (Taiwanese):**
- **Meaning:** 有效 (Effective), 按照实际情况 (In accordance with actual situations).
- **Example:** "此人格言虽好，但他所说的话并无效用，不按照实际情况来做事情。" (Though this person's words of wisdom are good, his words have no effect; one should not act according to them based on the actual situation.)
**Bilingual Slang Explanation:**
In Cantonese and Hokkien dialects, "valid" can be translated using phrases like "有效" (effective) or "有冇嘅效用" (does it have any effect) to mean that something is usable, genuine, or effective according to the current context or rules.
