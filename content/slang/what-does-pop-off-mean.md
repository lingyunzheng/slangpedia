```markdown
## English: Pop Off
### Chinese: 炸场 / 开挂 / 出彩
**Explanation:**
- **炸场 (zhà chǎng):** To perform or act in a way that creates a big impact, making an exciting or impressive show. Often used in the context of performances, games, or social events where someone's actions or skills suddenly become the highlight.
- **开挂 (kāi guà):** Originally referring to using cheats in video games, it has evolved to describe someone achieving something impressive or being particularly skilled or successful in a way that seems unfair or out of the norm. 
- **出色 (chū cǎi):** Literally meaning "out-of-paint" or "colorful," it suggests that someone is exceptionally impressive or outstanding in something they do.
**Example Usage:**
- *English:* He really popped off at the talent show by performing a flawless dance routine.
- *Chinese:* 他在才艺表演中炸场了，表演了一段完美的舞蹈。
- *English:* She popped off by acing the exam without studying.
- *Chinese:* 她开挂了，没有复习就考了高分。
- *English:* His soccer skills are popping off; he always scores in big games.
- *Chinese:* 他的 soccer 技巧炸场，总是在重要的比赛中进球。
```
This explanation covers the various connotations and examples of how the slang "pop off" is used in both English and Chinese.
