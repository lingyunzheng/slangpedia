## English: Real
### Chinese: 真实的 / 现实的
**Explanation:**
- **English Slang:** "Real" is used to describe something that is genuine, true, or not fake. It can also express emotional authenticity or truthfulness in a situation.
- **Chinese Slang:** "真实的" (zhēnzhě de) means genuine or true, while "现实的" (shíxìng de) can mean that something is practical or exists in the real world. In slang, it can emphasize the intensity of an emotion or a situation.
**Example:**
- **English:** "That's real cool!" -> "那真是太酷了！" (Nàguɑn zhēnshì tài kù le!)
- **Chinese:** "这个箱子里真的有宝！" (Zhè kè xiānglǐ zhēnshì yǒu bǎo!) -> "There's really something special in this box!"
## English: Real
### Chinese: 真正的 / 实际的
**Explanation:**
- **English Slang:** "Real" can also imply something is significant, important, or impactful. It can be used to stress the seriousness or the depth of a matter.
- **Chinese Slang:** "真正的" (zhuāngzhēn de) indicates something that is genuine or true, and "实际的" (shíchí de) means something that is real or practical. Both can be used to emphasize the depth or seriousness of an issue.
**Example:**
- **English:** "I feel real stressed today." -> "我今天真的好紧张。" (Wǒ jīntiān zhēnshì hǎo jǐnɡzhānɡ.)
- **Chinese:** "这是一次真正重要的会议。" (Zhè shì yī cì zhuānɡzhēn zhònɡjiào de huìyì.) -> "This is a really important meeting."
## English: Real
### Chinese: 真实的 / 来真的
**Explanation:**
- **English Slang:** "Real" often conveys a sense of "for real" or "genuine," suggesting that something is not a joke or exaggeration but is genuine or significant.
- **Chinese Slang:** "真实的" (zhēnzhě de) means genuine or true. "来真的" (lái zhēn de) is an idiom that means to be serious or to get down to business (literally means "come true").
**Example:**
- **English:** "You're really doing this?" -> "你来真的吗？" (Nǐ lái zhēn de ma?)
- **Chinese:** "这真的是要走的路吗？" (Zhè zhēnshì shì yào zǒu de lù ma?) -> "Is this really the road we must take?"
